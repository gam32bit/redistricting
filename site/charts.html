Method & caveats

Counts come from a script-parsed text extraction of the division's published redacted PDF (3,106 pages). The PDF's embedded font maps “f”, “ff”, “fi” and “fl” to unused codepoints, so every extractor silently deletes them (“La ayette” for “Lafayette”); those glyphs were recovered from the source before any counting, and every figure here was recomputed against the repaired text.

The published export repeats one answer across whole groups of questions — identical across the elementary, middle and high school scenarios in 1,539 of 1,540 records, and between “where the Division does well” and “a wish for the Division” in 1,015 of 1,018 — so comment counts are per respondent, never per school level. Keyword counts are plain substring matches and do not capture stance: a “diversity” mention can support or oppose the plan. Every one of them can be re-run in the companion comment explorer.

This is a self-selected feedback survey, not a random sample: it measures who showed up, not the community as a whole. Respondents could name multiple schools, so school-community groups overlap. Percentages are rounded and may not sum to 100.
